# Ellingson Plumbing — Hiring the Second Person

You have the master. The next hire is the person who rides the van with them. This doc covers the choice (apprentice vs journeyman), what to pay, where to find them, and two job posts ready to publish.

Wages below are Grand Forks / Fargo market, October 2026, from salary aggregators and live local postings — starting points to set your offer against, not fixed figures.

---

## 1. Apprentice or journeyman? Hire the journeyman if cash allows.

| | Apprentice | Journeyman |
|---|---|---|
| Pay (Grand Forks) | ~$20–25/hr | ~$28–35/hr |
| Can run a job solo? | **No** — ND requires 1st–3rd-year apprentices under *direct* supervision | **Yes** — can be deployed alone and can supervise an apprentice |
| Time to a second crew | ~3 years (when they hit year 4) | **Immediate** — the moment you have a second van |
| What they free the master to do | Haul, prep, clean (master still on every job) | Diagnose/sell/run the business while the journeyman produces |

**The call:** your `LAUNCH-ECONOMICS.md` constraint is that a 1st–3rd-year apprentice keeps you a one-crew shop for ~3 years, because they can't run a job alone. A **journeyman breaks that ceiling** — they can take the overflow jobs solo the day you add van #2, and they can supervise a future apprentice so the master isn't chained to every call. The extra ~$8–10/hr buys real capacity, not just help.

- **If you can carry ~$30/hr loaded:** hire a **journeyman** now. It's the faster path to two revenue streams and it lets the master sell and run the shop instead of turning every wrench.
- **If cash is tight at launch:** hire an **apprentice** (~$20–22/hr), register them with the ND + MN apprentice program within 30 days, and recruit the journeyman the quarter you hit 4 jobs/day. The apprentice becomes your journeyman in year 4 and seeds crew #3.

Either way: **register any apprentice with the ND State Plumbing Board within 30 days of their start date** — hours worked before registration don't count toward their license ([ND rules](https://www.ndplumbingboard.gov/faq)).

## 2. Where to find them (ranked for Grand Forks)

The local labor market is **tight** — Precision Plumbing, Total Care Plumbing, and Lunseth Plumbing & Heating are all actively posting for plumbers/apprentices right now. That cuts two ways: candidates are scarce, but anyone unhappy at those shops is reachable with a better offer and the pitch that they're building something at a new, owner-run company.

1. **Indeed + ZipRecruiter** — where every Grand Forks plumbing hire is posted. Paid-sponsor the listing; organic gets buried. ([Indeed ND plumber jobs](https://www.indeed.com/q-plumber-l-north-dakota-jobs.html))
2. **Facebook** — Grand Forks jobs groups + ND plumbing/trades groups. Highest-response channel for local trades, and free. Post the ad + a photo of the (wrapped) van.
3. **The supply-house counter** — once your Northern Plumbing Supply / Winnelson account is open, the counter staff know every plumber in town and who's looking. Ask them directly.
4. **Northland Community & Technical College** (East Grand Forks) career services — the regional source for newly-trained apprentices. Call career services and ask to post.
5. **ND Apprenticeship office / ND Job Service** — for registering and sourcing apprentices through the state program.
6. **UA Local 9 (Fargo)** — union hall for experienced journeymen if you want that route (comes with union scale + rules; weigh against open-shop flexibility).
7. **Word of mouth + a referral bounty** — $500–1,000 to anyone (customer, the master's network, the apprentice) who refers a hire that stays 90 days. Trades hire through trust faster than through ads.

**Vetting, every candidate:**
- Verify the license live on the **ND State Plumbing Board** lookup (and MN DLI if they claim MN) — never take a license number on faith.
- A paid **ride-along / working interview** on a real job tells you more than any résumé.
- Two references from prior crews; ask specifically about **callbacks and showing up on time** — those are the two things that cost you money.
- Clean driving record (they'll drive the van) and a drug-screen policy consistent with your insurance.

## 3. Comp structure that keeps good people

Base hourly is table stakes here; the retention lever is the upside:

- **Performance pay:** a small % of completed-ticket revenue (e.g. 3–5% to the tech on jobs they run) aligns them with billable efficiency — the exact number your economics live on.
- **On-call pay** for carrying the 24/7 phone on rotation (flat stipend per week + the emergency multiplier).
- **Benefits that punch above a small shop:** the competitor posts list 401(k) + health. You don't need to match day one, but name a path to it — a simple SIMPLE IRA + a health stipend reads as "real company."
- **Tool/boot allowance** and paid license/CE renewal — cheap, and it signals you invest in them.
