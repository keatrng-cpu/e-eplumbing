# Ellingson Plumbing — Buying the Van

Your one van is your capacity and your rolling billboard. This is how to buy the right one without tying up cash you need for payroll and parts. Listings are a Grand Forks / Fargo snapshot from October 2026 — they go stale fast, so treat them as what's typical, call to confirm, and expect to buy on condition, not on this list.

---

## 1. What to buy, and the decision rules

**Priorities for a cash-tight ND plumbing startup, in order:** winter reliability → cargo for water heaters + tools → mechanic-friendliness/parts → standing headroom → cash preserved.

| Van | Strengths | Watch-outs | Buy when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chevy Express 2500** | Cheapest to buy and fix, bulletproof V8, **RWD or 4x4** (real winter traction), any rural mechanic can service it | Low roof — no standing inside; dated, thirstier | Cash is king; you can work out of a low-roof |
| **Ford Transit (mid/high roof)** | **Stand-up cargo**, modern, better mpg/ergonomics, AWD on newer units, the default modern service van | Pricier; some engine-year quirks — check records | You want one van you can truly work out of |
| **Ram ProMaster 2500/3500** | **Widest box + lowest load floor** (easiest water-heater loading), FWD traction is good in snow | Less long-term/rust track record here, fewer local mechanics | Loading heavy equipment daily matters most |

**Hard rules:**
- **Buy on condition and rust, not year.** Red River Valley road salt kills frames — check the undercarriage, brake/fuel lines, and rockers before anything else.
- **Pre-purchase inspection, every time.** $150 at a shop on a $15–30k purchase is the cheapest insurance you'll buy.
- Target **≤120k miles** on a gas V8/V6 with service records; walk from a van with no records.
- **High roof if it's your only van** — standing to work is worth real money when you live in it 50 hours a week.
- Skip the small vans (ProMaster City, Transit Connect) — they can't carry a 50-gallon water heater and a full tool load.

## 3. The pick

- **Best all-around value:** the **2016 Grand Forks Transit at ~$17,224** — stand-up cargo, local, used price. Get the PPI and check the rust; if it's clean, it's the one.
- **Cheapest viable + winter edge:** the **2015 Express 2500 4x4 at ~$12,990** — if cash is the hard constraint and you can work out of a low roof, 4x4 in the Valley is worth the tradeoff.
- **If you'd rather finance low-mileage:** the **2019 Express 2500 at ~$22,990 / 45k mi** from Twin City Motors — local dealer, serviceable anywhere, decade of life left.

Whichever you choose, budget on top of the purchase:
- **Shelving/bins:** ~$2,000–4,000
- **Wrap + signage:** ~$2,000–3,000 (this is marketing — "owner names on the truck" in 300 driveways a year)
- **Commercial auto insurance, in the LLC's name**

## 4. How to pay for it (the tax lever)

- **Finance to preserve cash** if you're buying newer — payroll and a parts float matter more month-one than owning the van outright. A used van at $15–25k over 4–5 years keeps $15k+ in the business.
- **Title it in the LLC and keep it 100% business** — mixed personal use wrecks the deduction and complicates an insurance claim.
- **Section 179 / bonus depreciation** lets you deduct the van and the shelving/tools in year one when it's all business use. A heavier cargo van (GVWR over 6,000 lb — the 2500/3500 class) generally gets more favorable first-year treatment than a light vehicle. Confirm the current year's limits and your eligibility with your accountant before you count on it.

**Rule:** buy the van the business can service fully (3+ jobs/day) and pay for out of cash flow — not the nicest van the loan will approve. The van is capacity, not a trophy.
